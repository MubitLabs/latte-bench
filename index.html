EXPERIMENT SETUP

Plan. Pour. Reflect.

One 21-parameter plan per attempt. Execution runs at full speed without intervention. Each attempt then returns its foam map and scores for reflection.

Invalid plans consume an attempt. Notes are written after execution; only the selected memory ablation reaches the next plan.

Checking gateway…

Two model calls per attempt: plan and reflection, even for no-notes controls. Rejected plans count. Full traces are saved by the local server. Stopping prevents future attempts; an in-flight attempt may finish and be billed.

Try without an API

Replay the calibrated plan on default milk, with no model calls. The whole fixed schedule executes at full speed.

Heart

HOW TO PLAY
A steady hand. A good pour.
  1. Grind, then extract. Prepare 18 g of coffee and a 36 ml espresso.
  2. Steam to 55–65°C. Press Steam again to stop. Above 75°C, the milk scorches.
  3. Pick up the pitcher. Drag on Your cup to aim. Pour high to fill, then lower the spout to draw.
  4. Hold to pour. Tilt sets the flow. Aim for 190–240 ml with less than 5 ml spilled.
  5. Serve & score. Compare the actual foam surface with your selected target.

Simulation and evaluation limits
Rapier handles rigid-body collisions. Milk uses ballistic droplets and a 2D surface-fluid approximation, not full 3D CFD. Foam retention and mixing are heuristics: there is no density-driven crema/microfoam layering, surface tension, or calibrated rheology. Results measure control in this simulator, not real barista skill. Scoring measures geometry and task constraints, not taste or human-rated artistic quality. API decisions pause simulation time, and timed actions advance at a fixed 60 Hz.
Score changes across a few episodes are not evidence of weight updates. Use independent baselines and held-out targets alongside feedback practice; gateway adapter activation needs separate verification.
